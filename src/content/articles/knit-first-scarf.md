A scarf is a strong first knitting project because it's a rectangle. No shaping, no fitting, and no row-by-row pattern once you understand the stitch. Cast on, knit until it's long enough, bind off. The skills you practice (casting on, the knit stitch, binding off) are the foundation for everything else you'll ever knit.

## What you need

Choose light, solid-colored worsted yarn. The [beginner yarn guide](/articles/best-yarn-for-beginners/) explains why visible stitches help. Acrylic or a wool/acrylic blend is an option; check the actual yarn's washing instructions. Buy by the length needed for your chosen size, not by a generic promise that one skein makes a scarf. See the quantity example below and allow yarn for practice, finishing, and any fringe.

Look for a smooth, tightly plied worsted yarn rather than a fuzzy, splitty, or highly textured one. Avoid black, navy, dark brown, and any fluffy yarn for the first project. The stitches need to be visible.

US 8 (5.0 mm) straight needles, 10 or 14 inches long, are a useful starting point for worsted yarn whose label recommends that size. Choose a length that holds the stitches comfortably. Smooth wood or bamboo is worth trying if stitches slide off; metal is also suitable from the start. Finish, yarn, and your grip matter. The [needle size guide](/articles/needle-size-for-beginners/) explains the choices.

Scissors, a blunt tapestry needle for weaving in tails, and a tape measure or ruler. A crochet hook is useful for the optional fringe and for recovering dropped stitches.

## Choosing your stitch pattern

The instructions below make a garter-stitch scarf worked flat. Seed stitch is an alternative if you already know how to purl; use its repeat instead of the all-knit rows.

**Garter stitch** (knit every row) is the simplest fabric possible. Every stitch, every row, the same motion. The fabric has horizontal ridges, is squishy, reversible, and lies flat. If you only know the knit stitch, this is your pattern.

**Seed stitch** alternates knit and purl stitches, placing a knit above a purl and a purl above a knit as they face you. For 30 stitches worked flat: row 1, repeat knit 1, purl 1 to the end; row 2, repeat purl 1, knit 1 to the end. Repeat these two rows. With an odd count, alternate knit 1, purl 1 and end with knit 1 on every row. Move the yarn between the needle tips: back for knit, front for purl. These repeats include the edges; do not add the optional slipped garter edge to them.

Stockinette (knit one row, purl one row when worked flat) naturally curls at the edges. For this first scarf, garter or seed stitch avoids that characteristic curl without a separate border. Blocking is not a permanent cure for stockinette curl.

## How many stitches to cast on

Needle size does not guarantee gauge. Make a garter-stitch swatch with your yarn and needles, larger than the area you will measure. Wash and dry it according to the yarn instructions, then measure across the middle without stretching. Use that stitch gauge, not a label's stockinette gauge. If your garter gauge is 5 stitches per inch, the following are arithmetic examples:

- 20–25 stitches: 4–5 inches wide.
- 30–35 stitches: 6–7 inches wide.
- 40–45 stitches: 8–9 inches wide.

For example, 30 ÷ 5 = 6 inches. All 30 stitches are included, including both edges; no extra border stitches are added. At a different gauge, divide the stitch count by your stitches per inch. Swatch in seed stitch instead if that is your chosen fabric.

You can start with 30 as a practice count and check the width after several rows. If it is unsuitable, restart with a different count. Choosing 28 or 32 at the start is fine; keep that chosen number constant as you knit so the scarf stays rectangular.

## Holding the yarn

Two main styles, and you'll naturally drift toward one or the other.

**English (yarn in right hand):** the right hand wraps yarn around the needle for each stitch. Often called "throwing."

**Continental (yarn in left hand):** the right needle picks the yarn from the left hand. Often called "picking." Choose by comfort rather than a promised speed advantage.

Watch a five-minute video of each before starting. Pick the one that looks more comfortable. Either works. Don't worry about choosing the "best" style. Plenty of expert knitters use English, plenty use Continental, the finished work looks identical.

Wrap the yarn through your fingers somehow so it doesn't dangle loose. The most common setup loops it once around the pinky and over the index finger. Test on a few stitches and adjust. Tight wrapping makes stitches tight; loose wrapping makes them sloppy.

## Casting on

The long-tail cast on is one option. If it is new to you, follow [Purl Soho's illustrated long-tail tutorial](https://www.purlsoho.com/create/long-tail-cast-on/) for the slip knot, hand position, and repeated movements; this paragraph is only a setup overview. As a starting estimate, leave a tail 3–4 times the planned edge width plus about 6 inches for weaving in. For a 6-inch edge, that is 24–30 inches. Check that a usable tail remains after casting on. The slip knot counts as the first stitch.

Alternatively, use a knitted cast on. Make a slip knot on the left needle, leaving about 6 inches of tail. Draw a new knit loop through it using the ball yarn, but keep the original loop on the left needle. Place the new loop on the left needle without twisting it. Repeat into the newest loop until there are 30 loops in total, including the slip knot. The [Craft Yarn Council's illustrated instructions](https://media.craftyarncouncil.com/node/29) show this transfer and the knit stitch below.

Either method works. The scarf doesn't care which one you used.

After casting on, count. Move stitches along the needle one at a time. If the number's off by one or two, add or remove a stitch. Easier now than discovering it on row 40.

## Knitting the scarf

Work flat: knit every stitch in every row, including the first and last stitch. Turn after each row. If using a circular needle instead of straight needles, still turn; do not join the ends into a round.

Hold the needle with stitches in your left hand and keep the yarn from the ball behind the work. Insert the right needle through the front of the first loop from left to right. Bring the working yarn under and over the right tip, draw the new loop forward through the old loop, then slide only the old loop off the left needle. Repeat across. At the row end, turn the work so the other face is toward you and put the full needle in your left hand. Bring the working yarn to the back between the tips, not over a needle, and begin the next row.

The first few rows will be slow and awkward. Normal. After enough repetition, your hands start finding a rhythm. Save TV knitting for later, once you can form stitches without looking at every movement.

### Reading what you've made

A knit stitch looks like a small V from the front. Garter stitch makes horizontal ridges: one ridge represents two knitted rows. Count ridges on one face, not both faces added together. Twenty complete ridges represent forty rows; they do not imply a particular length without a row gauge.

For the knit method above, the right leg of each unworked loop should sit in front of the needle. If a loop is mounted the other way, support it with the other needle and remount it before knitting. This does not undo a twisted stitch already knitted lower down; undo back to that stitch if you want to correct it. Do not leave a live loop unsupported.

### Common beginner issues

**Stitches keep increasing.** You're accidentally creating new stitches, usually by wrapping the yarn over the needle at the start of a row. Before knitting the first stitch, make sure yarn hangs below and behind the needle, not draped over the top.

**Edges look messy.** First try knitting all edge stitches with even tension. For the optional slipped garter edge, bring the yarn to the back between the tips, slip the first stitch purlwise without knitting it, then knit all remaining stitches, including the last. Repeat every row. Slipping replaces knitting the first stitch; the total stays at 30. Keep the same edge method throughout.

**Tension is uneven.** Keep forming loops on the full shaft of the needle without pulling them tight. Practice on the swatch. Washing as the label allows can relax the fabric, but it does not repair added, dropped, or twisted stitches.

**Splitting the yarn.** The needle has caught only some plies. Undo that stitch and enter the whole loop. Slow down and check the tip's path; if a sharp tip keeps piercing the yarn, try a rounder tip or a smoother yarn, then check gauge again.

**Stitch count drifted.** Count after every row at first, then regularly, for example every ten rows. From 30 to 31 means a net increase; from 30 to 29 means a net loss, which could be a dropped stitch or an accidental decrease. Look for the cause before continuing. Secure a dropped loop immediately; do not hide the problem by adding a replacement stitch.

## How long to make it

As possible adult length targets, try 50–60 inches, about 40 inches for a shorter scarf, or 70–80 inches for a longer one. These are choices, not standard sizes or guarantees of a particular number of wraps. Compare a scarf you like and allow for the washed swatch's change in size.

Don't measure obsessively. Knit until the scarf is as long as you want it. Hold it up, try wrapping it around your neck. When it feels right, stop.

For a sourced comparison, Liz Chandler's [Basic Scarf in Garter Stitch](https://purlsandpixels.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/beginner-knit-scarf-revised-2019.pdf) estimates 304.5 yards of Caron Simply Soft for its 7 × 60-inch standard scarf. Its materials list asks for two 315-yard skeins: 630 yards purchased, not 630 yards consumed. Follow that original pattern if using its quantity; it is not a guarantee for this guide's 30-stitch scarf or a different yarn. For your own size, use a comparable pattern or estimate from a swatch in the same fabric, adding yarn for the swatch, tails, and fringe. Match dye lots when given. Before the final rows, leave yarn for a complete bind-off row and a 6-inch tail; if short, undo a row instead of cutting the yarn too soon.

Use the [yarn quantity guide](/articles/how-much-yarn-do-i-need/) for the swatch-based calculation and rounding up to whole skeins.

### Joining a new ball

When the first skein runs low, join the next ball at the start of a row if you can. Drop the old yarn, pick up the new yarn, leave a 6-inch tail on both. Knit the row with the new yarn. Tighten the first stitch once you have a few rows of new yarn. The [yarn joining guide](/articles/join-new-ball-of-yarn/) covers cleaner methods, but for a first scarf the drop-and-pick-up approach is fine.

## Binding off

When the scarf is long enough, bind off. Knit two stitches. Using the left needle, lift the first stitch over the second and off the right needle. Knit one more. Lift the previous stitch over. Repeat until one stitch remains. Cut the yarn (leave a 6-inch tail), pull the tail through the last loop, snug it up.

Don't bind off too tightly. A tight bind off makes the end narrower than the body, which looks odd. If yours tends tight, use a needle one or two sizes larger for the bind-off row. The classic beginner test: stretch the bind off edge by hand. If it stretches as much as the rest of the fabric, the bind off is loose enough.

## Finishing

Weave in every tail: the cast-on and bind-off tails plus both tails at each yarn join. With a blunt needle, follow the garter bumps on one face for about 2 inches, then change direction through neighboring bumps for another inch without pulling the fabric tight. Check both faces and gently stretch the fabric before trimming. Slippery yarn may need a longer path; test on the swatch. Ends are not guaranteed to be invisible or secure with every yarn after a fixed distance.

Finish according to the actual yarn label. If hand washing and flat drying are allowed, wash at the specified temperature, gently remove excess water without wringing, and lay the scarf flat to its intended shape without stretching. Let it dry fully. Test the treatment on your swatch first. Do not assume that every wool or acrylic yarn needs soaking, steaming, or an overnight drying time.

### Optional fringe

For a more traditional finish, add fringe to the short ends. Cut 12-inch strands of the scarf yarn, fold each in half, pull the loop through an edge stitch with a crochet hook, then pull the tails through the loop and tighten. Add a tassel every 1–2 stitches across both ends. Trim flush after attaching all of them, since cut lengths vary slightly.

## Between sessions

Between sessions, move live stitches away from the tips and use needle protectors if available. Stitches can slip off even at a row end. Keep the work in a closed project bag away from direct sunlight.

Keep your grip relaxed, take short breaks, and stop or change activities if pain, tingling, or numbness develops. Persistent, worsening, or neurological symptoms such as numbness warrant professional evaluation.

## FAQ

**How long does a first scarf take?**
There is no reliable average for this project: length, width, yarn, and practice all affect the time. Work in comfortable sessions rather than aiming for an hourly deadline.

**Can I use a different yarn weight?**
Yes. Choose needles recommended for that yarn, swatch in garter stitch, and calculate the cast-on count from your desired width. Recheck the quantity too; a thicker yarn does not make the same stitch count produce the same scarf.

**What if I make a mistake?**
A cosmetic irregularity can stay; secure a dropped live stitch before continuing. If you drop a stitch and notice immediately, slip it back onto the left needle. If a stitch unravels several rows down, the [dropped stitches guide](/articles/fix-dropped-stitches/) covers laddering it back up with a crochet hook.

**My yarn broke mid-scarf. What do I do?**
Treat it as a yarn join. If you have enough length, tink back to the start of the row and join the new yarn there. If not, join where the break happened, leave a 6-inch tail on both ends, and weave them in during finishing. Don't rely on a knot to hold the scarf together. Undo enough stitches to leave a usable tail before joining at a break.

**What's the difference between a knit stitch and a purl stitch?**
A knit stitch shows a V on the face where it was knitted; its reverse shows a purl bump. For the standard purl method, hold the yarn in front and insert the right needle into the front of the loop from right to left. The [purl tutorial](https://www.purlsoho.com/create/purl-stitch/) shows the complete movement if you choose seed stitch.

**Can I knit while watching TV?**
Wait until you can form stitches reliably while checking your work before dividing your attention.

**What should I knit after a scarf?**
A dishcloth (same skills, different shape, introduces following a simple pattern) or a hat (introduces knitting in the round and basic decreases). Both build on what you've learned. Once you're comfortable with knit and purl, you'll have many more patterns to choose from.
